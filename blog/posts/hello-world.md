# Hello, World: Why I Started Writing

Every program a developer writes begins with the same ritual: `print("Hello, World")`. It's the smallest possible proof that a system is alive and listening. This post is mine — the first signal that this blog is live.

## Why bother blogging?

I spend most of my time thinking about backends: how data flows, where it lives, how systems stay up when things go wrong. A lot of that thinking never leaves my head or my notebook. Writing forces me to:

- **Understand things properly.** If I can't explain a concept in plain words, I probably don't understand it yet.
- **Leave a trail.** Future me (and maybe you) will hit the same problems I'm solving today.
- **Connect with people.** Some of the best conversations I've had started with a blog post.

## What to expect here

I'll mostly write about:

1. **Backend engineering** — APIs, databases, and the unglamorous work that keeps apps running.
2. **System design** — the trade-offs behind scalable architecture.
3. **The journey** — what it's like learning this stuff as a student in Accra, aiming to become a world-class engineer.

> "The best way to learn is to build, break, and then write about what broke."

That's the plan. Thanks for reading the first line of output. There's a lot more coming.
